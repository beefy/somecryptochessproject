The protocol in four moves

A game is the work.
A win is the proof.

The chain does not trust a matchmaker, an engine or a score. It trusts a transcript that anyone can replay, a pairing anyone can recompute, and a game hash that has never appeared on chain before.

  1. 01

    Commit

    Miners register a bond and publish availability for the epoch ahead. A hashed nonce fixes their contribution before the beacon exists.

  2. 02

    Pair

    The beacon mixes chain history with the revealed nonces — and optionally a drand signature. It deterministically pairs the active roster.

  3. 03

    Play

    Two UCI engines play a legal game from a beacon-selected opening. Both seats co-sign the transcript; the relay clocks every move.

  4. 04

    Mine

    Only the winner earns a credit. Enough wins from distinct opponents, with previously unseen games, assemble into a block.

Why the work is real

No shortcuts, no trusted referee.

A legal PGN is not proof. The protocol binds every game to a pairing, an opening and two signing identities — then checks the result without asking how well anyone played.

◇

Previously unseen games

A canonical game hash excludes PGN metadata, clocks and comments. Nodes reject a game that has already appeared on the canonical chain.

⌁

Nobody picks their opponent

Pairings are a deterministic function of the epoch beacon. Miners commit before the beacon exists, so they cannot choose who they play.

↯

A relay cannot cheat

The matchmaker is untrusted. It cannot invent a pairing, change an opening or fabricate a result without the proof failing validation.

◎

Post-quantum custody

Wallet keys combine Ed25519 with ML-DSA-65 and sign with both. The SCCP leg is post-quantum; the Ethereum leg of the bridge is not.

≋

Bonds, not blind trust

Registration bonds, pairing diversity, co-signatures and win-only payment make self-play and collusion expensive rather than invisible.

◐

Designed to be inspected

Protocol formats are normative, every check is decidable from the record, and every page can be replayed by a full node.

The honest version

A prototype, not a promise.

What this is

  • A research prototype exploring whether useful adversarial computation can secure a chain.
  • A testbed for verifiable random matchmaking and commit-reveal beacons.
  • A public source of real, human-reviewable chess games.

What this is not

  • A drop-in replacement for Bitcoin, or proof that chess is a verifiable delay function.
  • A chess GUI, an engine or a rating system.
  • Anonymous, audited or ready for real money.
